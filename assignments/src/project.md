# Course Project Specification

## Project Overview

As a core component of this course, each student is required to
complete a course project. The project gives you an opportunity to
explore a specific topic related to AI and data systems in greater
depth, using AI coding tools to support your design, implementation,
and evaluation.

## Requirements

### Project Presentation

Each team will prepare a 6–8 minute presentation to share its project
with the class. The exact presentation length is tentative and may be
adjusted based on the final number of teams.

Your presentation should be accessible to all students, including
those who may not be familiar with your specific topic. You are
highly encouraged to include a video-recorded demo in your final
presentation.

### Final Report

Each team will submit a final report summarizing its project. The report should include:

* an introduction to the project topic,
* a summary of the system design,
* implementation details,
* evaluation results and key findings, and
* a conclusion discussing the implications of your work for AI / data systems.

There is no strict length requirement for the final report, but it
should be complete, well-structured, and clearly written.

## Project Presentation Format

The final project presentations will include an in-class lightning
talk and a shorter video-recorded demo submitted as part of the final
deliverables. You are encouraged to include an accessible link to
your demo video in your slides to showcase your project. Other
live-demo options are also welcome, such as providing an accessible
URL to a website or application that you have built and hosted for
demonstration purposes.

Since there are around 23 teams, the presentations will be split
across two days:

1. Thursday, June 11
2. Friday, June 12

The first day will accommodate around 12 teams, and the remaining 11
teams will present on the second day. On each day, we expect to have
approximately 6 presentations during the first hour and 5–6
presentations during the second hour, with a 10-minute break in the
middle.

**If your team has any availability constraints, please let me know
as soon as possible.**

I encourage you to attend in person if possible, though virtual
attendance remains an option for those with constraints.

Following a format commonly used in online conferences, each team
will have 6–8 minutes for the in-class lightning talk, followed by
2–4 minutes for Q&A. We will give a reminder at the 6-minute mark.
Please be mindful of the timing when preparing your slides.

To ensure smooth transitions between teams, please **submit your
final slide PDF on Canvas by Wednesday, June 10, at 5:00 PM**. We
will use the instructor’s computer for all presentations.

Your slides should be in widescreen format, either 16:9 or 13.33 in ×
7.5 in.

## Assessment Criteria

Your project will be assessed based on the following criteria:

### Depth of Understanding

Does your project demonstrate a thorough understanding of the chosen topic?

### Quality of Deliverables

* **Demonstrable MVP:** How complete, robust, practical, and efficient is your system?
* **Complete report:** Is your report well-structured, clearly written, and properly referenced?
* **Presentation and demo:** Can you effectively communicate and pitch your idea and product to the class? Is the presentation engaging, and is the demo compelling?
